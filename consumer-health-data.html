Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy

Lever and Lever Coach · Version 1.0 · Effective 8 November 2026

This page supplements our Privacy Policy. It is written for the Washington My Health My Data Act (RCW 19.373), Nevada SB 370 and similar state laws, and applies to residents of those states and to anyone whose consumer health data is collected in those states.

In short

  • Only Lever asks health questions (injuries, heart condition, pregnancy, pain after a workout). Lever Coach does not.
  • Lever keeps and uses them only if you say yes on a separate screen, and only to adjust your training program. If you say no, the answers are deleted.
  • We also treat your body weight, height and age as consumer health data. Lever needs them to build the program you asked for.
  • They stay on your phone unless you sign in. Then they are stored by our database provider in Ireland. On iPhone, your own iCloud backup, if you turned it on, contains the app's data too.
  • We do not share them with your coach, with analytics companies, or with anyone else. We do not sell them.
  • You can withdraw consent, get a copy, and delete them at any time.

2. What consumer health data we collect, why, and how we use it

In the Lever app only, and kept only if you consent, we collect:

  • Injury and medical answers: joint pain, a heart condition, pregnancy.
  • Pain reports: any pain you report after a workout.

Purpose and use: to adjust your training program, by swapping exercises and lowering loads. We use these answers for nothing else: not for advertising, not for statistics, not for research, not to train any model.

We also treat your body weight, height and age as consumer health data. Lever needs them to build the program you asked for, so no separate consent is required for them. They stay on your phone or, if you sign in, in our database in Ireland; no coach sees them and no analytics service receives them. We do not use any of your data to infer a health condition.

Lever Coach does not ask for consumer health data. If a coach writes health information about a client in their own notes, we store it only for the coach, as a processor; the coach is responsible for it.

3. Where the data comes from

Only from you: the answers you type or tap in the Lever app. We do not get health data from your coach, from other apps, from data brokers or from any other source.

4. How we collect it: consent

Lever first shows a one-time notice that it is not a medical service. It then asks an optional question: may your injury and medical answers and any pain you report after a workout be used to adjust your program? If you say no, those answers are deleted and the program is built without them. If you say yes, they are saved. Saying no does not limit any other feature of the app.

Your body weight, height and age are asked in the sign-up quiz because the program cannot be built without them (RCW 19.373.030(1)(a): necessary to provide the service you requested).

5. What consumer health data we share, and with whom

We do not share consumer health data with any third party. In particular, it is never sent to:

  • your coach (section 9 of the Privacy Policy lists what a coach sees; health answers are not on that list);
  • Amplitude, Google Firebase or any other analytics or crash-reporting service;
  • RevenueCat or Google. Apple holds your iCloud backup only if you turned it on; it is kept in your Apple account under Apple's policy, and we cannot read it;
  • advertisers, data brokers or affiliates. We have no affiliates.

Processor. If you are signed in, the answers are stored by Supabase, Inc., our database provider, in Ireland (EU). Supabase stores them on our behalf under a contract that limits its use to hosting, and is not allowed to use them for its own purposes. Nobody else processes them.

Sale. We do not sell consumer health data and will not sell it. We would ask for your separate written authorization before any sale, as the law requires; we have no plan to ask.

Geofencing. We do not use geofencing around any health care facility or anywhere else.

We would disclose consumer health data only if a valid law-enforcement request or court order forced us to, and only to the extent required.

6. How long we keep it

Health answers: until you delete your account or ask us to delete them; without an account, until you clear the app's data. Body weight, height and age: until you delete your account; without an account, until you clear the app's data. On Android, backup of the app's data is turned off, so nothing goes to your Google backup. On iPhone, your iCloud backup, if turned on, contains the app's data; Apple keeps it under your Apple account. We do not keep database backups.

7. Your rights and how to exercise them

  • Confirm and access: ask whether we hold consumer health data about you, get a copy, and get the list of anyone we shared it with (nobody, see section 5).
  • Withdraw consent: turn the health consent off in Lever's settings at any time. Lever then stops using the answers. Answers already saved are deleted when you delete your account, or within 30 days of your e-mail request.
  • Delete: delete your account in the app (Settings, Delete account), or by e-mail to us from the address of your account. Without an account, clear the app's data. We delete within 30 days, including from any processor. We keep no database backups, so there is nothing to delete from them.

To exercise a right by e-mail, write to this.is.daily.staff@gmail.com from the e-mail address of your Lever account. We answer within 45 days. If we need more time, we tell you why and take up to 45 more days. We do not charge for requests. We never discriminate against you for using your rights.

Appeal. If we refuse your request, reply to our answer with the word "Appeal" in the subject. We review the appeal and answer in writing within 45 days with the reasons. If you still disagree, you can contact the Washington Attorney General (atg.wa.gov), the Nevada Attorney General, or the attorney general of your state; our answer will say how.

8. Changes

We will not collect new categories of consumer health data, or use or share it for a new purpose, without first updating this page and asking for your consent again. This page carries its version and effective date at the top.
